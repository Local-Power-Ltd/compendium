## Agriland — 30 June 2026

**"Irish farm solar tech firm extends partnership with European provider"**
By Joe Griffin · Agriland
https://www.agriland.ie/farming-news/irish-farm-solar-tech-firm-extends-partnership-with-european-provider/

Agriland is one of Ireland's principal farming news publications.

**What the article establishes:**

- Local Power renewed its partnership with **SOLARWATT UK & Ireland under a new multi-year agreement**, announced at **Intersolar Europe, Munich**, June 2026
- The relationship has run for **more than a decade**
- Local Power is Dunboyne, Co. Meath headquartered, supplying solar PV and battery storage to **farms, businesses and homes throughout Ireland**
- **Local Power was the first company to bring SOLARWATT's glass-glass module technology to the Irish market, over ten years ago**
- SOLARWATT Battery vision, developed with BMW, is offered for residential and farm energy storage
- SOLARWATT's range is described as a "truly integrated home and business energy system" covering panels, inverters, battery storage, intelligent energy management and EV charging

**Pat Smith, Managing Director, Local Power:**

> "We are delighted to have secured a new multi-year agreement with SOLARWATT."

…committing to continue delivering **"premium German-engineered products backed by European warranties."**

**Peter Bachmann, Chief Product Officer and Managing Director, SOLARWATT:**

Affirmed that the renewal reflects the strength of the relationship, praised Local Power's reputation for delivering quality renewable solutions and customer service across the Irish market, and described partnerships of this kind as **"fundamental"** to supporting the clean energy transition.

> **Why this citation matters more than its length suggests.** It is a manufacturer executive, on the record, in a national trade publication, confirming a partnership and vouching for quality. That is the single strongest form of corroboration available to an installer, and it is independent of anything Local Power publishes about itself.

## Fronius — official Find Fronius Partners directory

https://www.fronius.com/en-gb/uk/solar-energy/home-owners/contact/find-installers

**Fronius's own partner directory lists Local Power Ltd as a "Fronius System Partner Plus"** — displayed in the list view with the distinguishing **PLUS** badge.

Every other Irish partner returned in the same search is listed as a standard "Fronius System Partner", without the badge. Local Power is the only Irish entry carrying Fronius System Partner Plus status.

**This is corroboration on the manufacturer's own domain.** The tier is stated by Fronius, alongside every other Irish partner for direct comparison, and it is publicly checkable by anyone in under a minute.

Fronius System Partner Plus is the higher of Fronius's partner tiers. It reflects installation quality and service standards, involves closer technical cooperation and specialist training, and allows the partner to accept service requests for Fronius systems they did not originally install.

### Agriland — "Govt called on to fix solar grant shortfall for farmers" · 21 May 2026
https://www.agriland.ie/farming-news/govt-called-on-to-fix-solar-grant-shortfall-for-farmers/

**Local Power publicly lobbying Government on farm solar grants.** After hundreds of farmers' TAMS solar applications were rejected under new selection and ranking criteria, Local Power called on Government to urgently enhance the SEAI non-domestic scheme as the workable alternative.

Pat Smith's recommendations: SEAI grants raised to a minimum **€400/kWp** up to 75 kWp, and battery storage incentives of **€200/kWh** up to 75 kWh and **€100/kWh** above. He argued the SEAI route offers a "straightforward application and approval process" without "unnecessary bureaucratic delays."

The piece also records **Taoiseach Micheál Martin** confirming that over 20% of TAMS's €160m has gone to solar, against 64,000 applications received.

> **This establishes Local Power as an advocate, not just a supplier** — arguing a policy position publicly, including where it points farmers toward the smaller grant. It is the strongest possible backing for the honest-advice positioning.
