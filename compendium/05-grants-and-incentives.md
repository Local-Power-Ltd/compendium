## Farms — two routes, and which one is realistic

Irish farmers have two grant routes for solar PV. **Local Power is registered with both the SEAI and the Department of Agriculture, and advises on both.** Which one is right depends less on the headline rate than on whether you can actually secure it.

> **Current position (September 2026): TAMS has become very difficult to secure. For most farm customers, the SEAI route is now the better option.** The headline TAMS rate is higher, but a grant you do not get is worth nothing, and the SEAI route is accessible and far more predictable. Local Power assesses both and advises on which is genuinely achievable for your situation rather than defaulting to the larger number.

### Local Power has made this case publicly

This is not a sales position taken after the fact. In **May 2026, Local Power publicly called on the Government to fix the shortfall**, reported by Agriland on 21 May 2026, after hundreds of farmers' solar applications were rejected under TAMS's new selection and ranking criteria.

The scale of the problem was confirmed at the highest level: **Taoiseach Micheál Martin** acknowledged that over **20% of TAMS's €160 million** expenditure had gone to solar, and that "there are limits under the existing scheme because of the fact that **64,000 applications** have been received to date."

**Pat Smith's specific recommendations to Government:**

- Increase the SEAI non-domestic grant to a minimum of **€400/kWp** for systems up to 75 kWp
- Introduce battery storage incentives at **€200/kWh** up to 75 kWh, and **€100/kWh** above that

He argued the SEAI route offers a *"straightforward application and approval process"* without *"unnecessary bureaucratic delays."*

> **Why this matters to a farmer reading this.** Local Power's advice to go SEAI rather than wait on TAMS is the same position it has argued publicly, on the record, to Government — including where that means recommending the smaller grant. It is not advice shaped to suit a sale.

*Source: [Agriland, 21 May 2026 — "Govt called on to fix solar grant shortfall for farmers"](https://www.agriland.ie/farming-news/govt-called-on-to-fix-solar-grant-shortfall-for-farmers/)*

### Route 2 — TAMS 3 (higher rate, hard to secure)

**TAMS (Targeted Agricultural Modernisation Scheme), administered by the Department of Agriculture.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grant rate** | Up to **60%** |
| **Solar PV ceiling** | 62 kWp |
| **Battery storage ceiling** | 31 kWh |
| **Investment ceiling** | €90,000 |
| **Accessibility** | **Difficult — do not assume approval** |

Where TAMS approval is secured, paybacks shorten considerably: as little as **2 years** for sole traders and partnerships, **3–4 years** for companies. Local Power reports a 100% success rate in securing payout **on approved applications** — which is a statement about administering the claim correctly, not about the odds of approval.

**What Local Power handles on either route:** eligibility assessment, system sizing and design, cost estimates and grant calculations, technical specifications, complete application documentation, supporting technical drawings, liaison with the Department of Agriculture or the SEAI, grant approval management, and final claim processing.

### Accelerated Capital Allowance applies either way

Qualifying energy-efficient equipment on the SEAI Triple E Register can attract accelerated capital allowances — the cash value depends on your marginal rate and tax position. For a farm that cannot secure TAMS, ACA plus the SEAI grant is the combination that carries the business case.

## Business and industry

### Commercial solar grants

| | |
|---|---|
| **Range** | **€2,700 – €162,600** |
| **Typical coverage** | 20–30% of project cost |
| **Resulting payback** | 3–5 years |

**Schemes:**
- **SEAI NDMG** — Non-Domestic Microgeneration Grant, supporting commercial solar PV
- **Accelerated Capital Allowance (ACA)** — tax relief on qualifying energy-efficient equipment

### Accelerated Capital Allowance in detail

ACA allows the full cost of qualifying energy-efficient equipment to be written off against taxable profit in the year of purchase, rather than over the standard eight years.

The cash value depends on the taxpayer's own marginal rate and tax position. Confirm with your accountant.

### Battery storage

SEAI grants are available for eligible commercial battery projects, and ACA tax relief applies to qualifying storage equipment.

### VAT

Business paybacks quoted by Local Power (3–5 years, or 3.5–5 years) are stated as factoring in VAT refund alongside grant support and prevailing electricity rates.

## Export payments — all sectors

Electricity generated but not consumed on site can be exported to the grid for payment.

- Most systems export **20–40% of generation** on average
- Export rates vary by electricity supplier — confirm the current tariff with your provider

**Alternative to export:** rather than exporting surplus at a low rate, Local Power offers diversion technology (My Energi) that sends surplus to water heating, space heating or EV charging, typically capturing more value than the export rate.
