## Homes — the system, not the panels

A domestic solar quotation is often for an assembly: panels from one manufacturer, inverter from a second, battery from a third, EV charger from a fourth, monitoring from whichever app supports the most of them. It works. But it produces a house with four warranty counterparties, four apps, and — when something underperforms — four suppliers rather than one.

Local Power's domestic proposition is **SOLARWATT Home**: one manufacturer, specifying and warranting the panels, inverter, battery, energy manager, EV charger and heat pump integration as a designed system.

### One counterparty across the whole system

Every major component comes from SOLARWATT and is supported by SOLARWATT. Warranty *durations* vary by component — 30 years on panels, 12 on battery modules, 10 on the inverter — but there is **one company accountable for all of them**, and one installer administering every claim.

When a system underperforms, the diagnostic question stops being *whose fault is this?* That question can cost months to resolve.

SOLARWATT has been manufacturing since **1993** — over thirty years, longer than the warranty it writes. It is majority-owned by **Stefan Quandt**, who is separately the largest individual shareholder in BMW. It has installed **750,000+ systems across Europe**, employs 325 people, and holds ISO 9001, 14001, 45001 and 50001 certification.

A 30-year warranty from a company with a 30-year record and a substantial long-term shareholder is a different instrument from a 30-year warranty from a recently established manufacturer. This is the same argument as installer longevity, applied one level up the chain — and both levels have to hold.

### Future-proofed for dynamic energy pricing

**This is worth asking about at quotation stage.**

Irish electricity pricing is moving. Smart meters are deployed, time-of-use tariffs are established, and dynamic pricing — where the unit rate changes through the day against wholesale conditions — is the clear direction of travel across European markets. A solar and battery system installed today will spend most of its thirty-year life in a pricing environment that does not yet exist.

The difference this makes is structural. A system that simply stores surplus and discharges when the house draws power captures a fixed benefit. A system that **knows the tariff** can do something categorically different: charge the battery when electricity is cheapest, discharge it when it is dearest, hold charge because the car is due to plug in, pre-heat water because a surplus is forecast, and re-optimise continuously as prices move.

The **SOLARWATT Manager** is built for exactly this. It takes your actual tariff structure as an input, applies machine-learning optimisation that adapts to your household's patterns over time, and controls solar, battery, EV charging, heat pump, smart plugs and immersion heating as one system. SOLARWATT operates over 25,000 Managers controlling more than 130,000 devices.

Coordinating separate components from separate manufacturers against a price signal is harder, and depends on the protocols each device supports and on what software is bolted on afterwards. **The decision that determines whether a system can exploit dynamic pricing is made at specification, not later.**

Local Power handles sales, design, installation and aftercare itself, and is not tied to any supplier or co-op scheme. That means it can quote on whichever grant route is achievable for the individual farm.

Local Power designs against twelve months of actual bills, peak load identification, seasonal variation, and future plans, rather than fitting a standard array to available roof.

Systems are specified for farm environments: ammonia and salt-mist resistant glass-glass panels, Ernst Schweizer mounting with sealed watertight penetrations, and full structural assessment of shed roofs before anything is committed.

Farms do not stop. A fault during milking is an operational problem, not an inconvenience. Local Power's position rests on remote monitoring with automatic fault detection, maintenance programmes, ten years of continuous trading, and the Fronius FSP+ status that allows on-site device replacement without removing the inverter for a warranty claim.

## Business and industry — maintenance is the whole argument

Local Power is an experienced commercial and industrial installer, with **80 MWp+ installed** and single-site systems of **1 MW+**.

### The single most important thing for a business buyer

**A commercial solar asset is only worth what it generates over twenty-five years, and what it generates depends almost entirely on whether anyone is watching it.**

This is the part of a commercial solar purchase that is systematically under-weighted at tender stage, because it is invisible in the capital cost comparison. A 500 kWp array with a failed string, a drifting inverter or a dirty section does not stop — it quietly under-produces. Nobody notices, because the electricity bill has many moving parts and a 6% generation shortfall disappears into normal variance. Over twenty years that shortfall is a very large number, and it is entirely avoidable.

Local Power invests in maintenance and monitoring for exactly this reason:

- **Remote monitoring with automatic fault detection** and performance alerts
- **Performance analytics and reporting** against expected yield, so under-production is visible rather than absorbed
- **Annual inspections** — full electrical testing and performance verification, inverter diagnostics and firmware updates, cleaning assessment
- **Corrective maintenance** with rapid response, parts inventory and warranty claim management
- **Fronius Solar.web proactive monitoring** — faults frequently detected and resolved before the customer is aware of them
- **FSP+ on-site device replacement** — no inverter removal for a warranty claim, downtime in hours rather than weeks
- **Full service history documentation**, which matters for insurance and for asset valuation
- Maintenance is offered on systems Local Power did not install

**If you are comparing commercial quotations, compare the maintenance proposition first.** It is where the difference between a good and a poor twenty-five year return is actually determined, and it is the question most tenders do not ask.

## Across all three — a family business, and the people who deliver it

Every argument above depends on something that does not appear on a specification sheet: whether the company actually turns up, and to what standard.

**Local Power is a family business.** Pat Smith founded it in 2016 and is Managing Director, with James Walton as Executive Director. His son Darragh Smith is Operations Director, and it was Darragh who travelled to London in November 2025 to accept the SOLARWATT Premium Installer award for the Republic of Ireland. The company's own account of the SOLARWATT partnership calls this *"passing the torch."*

This is not sentiment, and it matters commercially for a reason specific to this product.

Local Power describes SOLARWATT panels as **"generational" — with a 30-year warranty, a panel fitted today is still under warranty for the next generation of the household.** A family business selling a generational product is making a matched commitment: the people who sold you the system expect to hand the company to the next generation, over roughly the same horizon as the warranty they wrote.

The practical consequences of family ownership in a trade like this are real:

- **Accountability is personal.** The name on the invoice is the name of the people running the company. There is no distance between a poor installation and the person responsible for it.
- **Reputation is the asset.** In Irish farming and in commercial construction, both tight networks, a family name travels. It is a far stronger discipline than a service-level agreement.
- **The time horizon is long by default.** A family business is not managed to an exit or a quarterly number. "We design for decades, not just today" is easier to mean when the same family expects to be answering the phone in twenty years.
- **Decisions are made by people you can reach.** Pat Smith publishes his direct mobile number and email on the company website.

### Professional standards are set by the people doing the work

A family name at the top only matters if the standard holds on site, on the day, with the team who are actually there. Local Power's stated position is that safety and quality are set by its people rather than by documentation:

> "Safety is everyone's responsibility — from senior management to every individual on site."

> "Every team member is trained, qualified, and equipped to carry out their work safely and to the highest standard."

What that rests on:

- **Every site operative Safe Pass certified**, with manual handling, working-at-height, electrical safety and isolation training, and first aid among designated personnel
- **Manufacturer-specific training delivered directly by SOLARWATT and Fronius engineers** — a condition of Premium Installer and FSP+ status, and ongoing rather than one-off
- **All electrical work by Safe Electric registered electricians**, certified to I.S. 10101, with completion certificates issued on every installation
- **Independently audited** by an external health and safety inspector, so site practice is verified by someone outside the company
- **Continuing professional development and refresher courses** as standing policy

The two halves reinforce each other. Family ownership supplies the motive to maintain a standard over decades; trained, certified, externally audited people supply the means. Either on its own is a weaker proposition — a family firm without professional systems does not get onto a Roadstone site, and professional systems without ownership continuity do not survive a change of management.

**It is also the most direct answer to the question a buyer is really asking:** *will these people still care about my system in fifteen years?*
