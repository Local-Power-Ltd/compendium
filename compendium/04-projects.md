## Multi-site frameworks and portfolio programmes

> **Portfolio delivery.** A single rooftop is a job. An estate of rooftops, sequenced around live operations with one design standard, one set of grid applications and one reporting line, is a different discipline — and it is where Local Power does a substantial share of its work. Five programmes are currently active.

| Programme | Client | Period | Scope | Delivered |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-site retail and plaza estate | Supermac's / The Plaza Group | 2022–ongoing | 900 kWp+ rooftop solar, design and install | 6 sites, further sites planned |
| National research centre programme | Teagasc (via eTenders) | 2023–ongoing | 685 kWp across five research centres | Ashtown, Athenry, Clonakilty, Johnstown Castle, Moorepark |
| Nursing home group programme | Evergreen, Mowlam Healthcare, Newbrook | 2024–ongoing | 650 kWp+ rolling rooftop and ground-mounted | Newbrook 6 homes, Evergreen 3, Mowlam 6 |
| Plant and machinery estate | Pat O'Donnell & Co | 2023–ongoing | 400 kWp+ rooftop solar | Dublin, Galway, Portadown, Cork |
| Motor and retail group estates | Connolly Motors, EZ Living, Moldovan Stores | 2021–ongoing | 1 MW+ across group estates | 24 sites and growing |

### How a portfolio programme runs

1. **Survey every site first** — structural and electrical surveys completed across the whole estate before any single site starts, so design is settled once rather than renegotiated site by site
2. **One design standard** — a single equipment and mounting specification across the estate, varied only where a particular roof demands it
3. **Applications in batches** — ESB (NC6, NC7, NC8) and SEAI Non-Domestic Microgeneration applications lodged together and tracked centrally
4. **Crews sequenced by region** — sites grouped geographically so crews move efficiently and multiple sites can run in parallel
5. **One reporting line** — a single project manager and one monitoring portal covering the whole portfolio

Up to three commercial installation crews operate in parallel, with up to three sites deliverable per week.

### Delivering on premises that cannot close

Across research centres, nursing homes, food production, retail, motor dealerships and transport infrastructure, the common constraint is the same: the building cannot stop for the installation.

- Buildings occupied around the clock, with no shutdown window
- Access, noise and craning agreed with each site manager in advance, not imposed
- Method statements and site-specific risk assessments issued before any crew goes on a roof
- Documentation, certification and grant paperwork produced to a standard that survives audit

1. **Blue-chip acceptance.** Toyota Ireland, Roadstone, Ardagh Packaging and Translink are organisations with formal procurement and technical due-diligence processes.
2. **Depth in food and dairy.** Lakeland Dairies, Greenfield Foods, Oakpark Foods and Dunleavy Meats represent a sector with 24-hour operation, heavy refrigeration load and strict compliance — technically the hardest solar and storage environment in Ireland.
3. **Public-sector and research validation.** Teagasc is Ireland's national agriculture and food development authority.
4. **Agricultural breadth.** Dairy, poultry, robotic dairy and mixed farms, consistent with the founder's IFA background.
5. **Scale range.** From domestic rooftop through 500 kWp to a 1 MW+ single-site installation, with 80 MWp+ delivered in total. Few Irish installers operate across that full range.
6. **Storage is live, not prospective.** Commercial and industrial battery storage projects are operational — relevant because a great many installers market C&I storage without having commissioned one.
7. **Clients come back.** Repeat business from clients including The Plaza Group, in a category most buyers purchase once.

### Curragh Biogas — Carnaross, Co. Meath

An agricultural biomethane plant developed by Local Power in partnership with **Biogest**, the Vienna-based Austrian anaerobic digestion specialist. **Confirmed by the Irish Farmers Journal, 19 March 2026**, and covered by the Irish Independent.

| | |
|---|---|
| Output | 599 Nm³/h biomethane |
| Feedstock | Poultry, cattle and pig slurry, plus grass, maize and rye silage |

| Status | Full planning permission |
| Model | Farmer-inclusive: feedstock supplied by local farmers |

*Sources: [Irish Farmers Journal, 19 March 2026](https://www.farmersjournal.ie/news/news/solar-supplier-expands-into-ad-with-new-partnership-908756); [Irish Independent](https://www.independent.ie/county/meath/anaerobic-digestion-project-planned-at-meath-plant-following-new-partnership/a/143000946.html); Bioenergy Insight. Curragh Biogas is Local Power's first joint project with Biogest under a partnership aimed at building a farmer-inclusive model of biomethane production in Ireland.
